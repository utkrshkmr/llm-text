\section*{Coverage of the source books}

where this book covers it. \textsf{LLM} is \emph{Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)}
(Raschka, 2024); \textsf{RM} is \emph{Build a Reasoning Model (From Scratch)} (Raschka). The
material is reorganised so that overlapping topics appear once. For example, LLM~5.3 (temperature
and top-$k$) and RM~4.4--4.5 (temperature and top-$p$) are both developed in
\cref{ch:pretrain,ch:scaling}, and each later chapter builds on the earlier one. The margin tags

\section*{Coverage of Stanford CS336 (Spring 2026)}

Part~VII is built around the five assignments of Stanford's \emph{CS336: Language Modeling from
Scratch} (\url{https://cs336.stanford.edu}). Every lecture topic that the two source books leave
out has a chapter of its own. Margin tags of the form \textsf{CS336 L$n$} (lecture) and
\textsf{CS336 A$k$ \S$m$} (assignment section) mark the coverage. The project guides deliberately
stop short of solutions: they describe each assignment in our own words, explain how to set it up,
and point, problem by problem, to the chapters and labs that teach what you need.

\section*{Coverage of the preference-optimization primer}

The treatment of preference optimization follows the structure of the primer by Aman Chadha
(\url{https://aman.ai/primers/preference-optimization/}), tagged \textsf{PO \S$n$} in the margins:
the RL refresher and the notation mapping between classical RL and language modelling, REINFORCE,
TRPO and PPO with GAE (\cref{ch:pg}); RLHF with reward and value models, the roles of the four models,
RLAIF and agentic RL (\cref{ch:rlhf}); DPO, KTO, IPO, ORPO, SimPO and related methods (\cref{ch:dpo});
GRPO and its successors DAPO, Dr.~GRPO, GSPO and CISPO (\cref{ch:grpo,ch:grpo2}); and the comparative
analysis across methods (\cref{ch:dpo}).

\subsection*{Chapter 39: RL Training Systems}

\paragraph{Exercise 39.1 (Optimal split).}
Let $x=G_{\text{gen}}$. $T(x)=W_g/x+W_t/(G-x)+T_{\text{sync}}$; setting $T'(x)=0$ gives
$W_g/x^2=W_t/(G-x)^2$, so $x^\star/G=\sqrt{W_g}/(\sqrt{W_g}+\sqrt{W_t})$ and
$T^\star=(\sqrt{W_g}+\sqrt{W_t})^2/G+T_{\text{sync}}=(W_g+W_t+2\sqrt{W_gW_t})/G+T_{\text{sync}}$.
Since $2\sqrt{W_gW_t}\ge0$, $T^\star\ge (W_g+W_t)/G=T_{\text{coloc}}-T_{\text{switch}}$ (ignoring
$T_{\text{sync}}$), with equality iff $W_gW_t=0$. The synchronous split wins iff
$T_{\text{switch}}>2\sqrt{W_gW_t}/G+T_{\text{sync}}$, i.e.\ only when switching is very expensive.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.2 (KV budget).}
Qwen3-4B has $\approx$4.02B parameters ($\approx$8.0\,GB in bf16). Its KV cost is the same as the 8B
model's, $2\cdot36\cdot8\cdot128\cdot2=147$\,KB per token, because layer count and KV heads match.
Budget $\approx 80-8.0-8=64$\,GB; at 6{,}000 live tokens per sequence, $64\text{e}9/(147\text{e}3\cdot
6000)\approx 72$ sequences. Each decode step streams $\approx72$\,GB, $\approx21.5$\,ms at 3.35\,TB/s,
so the bound is $\approx 72/0.0215\approx 3{,}400$ tokens/s per GPU---long contexts make KV, not
weights, the dominant traffic.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.3 (Staleness bound).}
Batch $k$ starts generating at time $\ge$ the completion of training step $k-s-1$, so at least
$k-s$ updates have been applied; its version $v\ge k-s$. It is consumed by training step $k$,
after which the policy has version $k+1$; the update to $\theta_{k+1}$ uses data from $\theta_v$,
staleness $k-v\le s$. With $s=0$, batch $k$ cannot start until step $k-1$ finishes, which is the
synchronous split schedule (\cref{fig:rlsys:timelines}a with separate pools).

\paragraph{Exercise 39.4 (Loss normalisation).}
(a) $(\ell_1+\ell_2)/2$, each micro-batch weighted equally regardless of size. (b)
$(100\ell_1+1000\ell_2)/1100$. Fix: before the accumulation loop all-reduce the total number of
loss tokens $N_{\text{glob}}$ over micro-batches and ranks, compute each micro-batch loss as
\code{(tok\_loss * mask).sum() / N\_glob}, and do not divide by the number of micro-batches or
ranks again (set the DDP gradient averaging accordingly, or multiply by the world size).

\paragraph{Exercise 39.5 (TIS bias).}
With $\log\rho\sim\mathcal{N}(-s^2/2,s^2)$ (so $\E[\rho]=1$),
$\E[\min(\rho,C)]=\Phi\!\big(\tfrac{\ln C-s^2/2}{s}\big)+C\big(1-\Phi\big(\tfrac{\ln C+s^2/2}{s}\big)\big)$.
For $C=2$ the bias $\E[\min(\rho,C)]-1$ is $\approx0$ at $s=0.1$, $-0.0015$ at $s=0.3$, $-0.026$ at
$s=0.5$ and $-0.19$ at $s=1$. Mismatch is usually small ($s\ll1$), so the bias is negligible while
the variance reduction from removing the heavy right tail is large; when $s$ is large the bias is a
warning that the engine and trainer disagree too much.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.6 (Packing efficiency).}
At most one bin in a first-fit packing is at most half full: if bins $i<j$ were both at most half
full, the items of $j$ would have fit into $i$ when they were placed. Hence with $k$ bins the total
size exceeds $(k-1)\cdot\text{cap}/2$, and $\mathrm{OPT}\ge$ total/cap gives $k<2\,\mathrm{OPT}+1$.
Empirically, padded efficiency falls quickly with $\sigma$ (e.g.\ $\approx$0.4 at $\sigma=0.8$ for
batches of 8), sorting recovers a large part, and FFD packing stays above 0.9.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.7 (Partial-rollout bias).}
Record \code{[r.length for r in fin]} per step. In the first steps the consumed mean length is well
below the generated mean (short responses finish first); as carried-over long responses complete,
later batches contain them and the consumed distribution approaches the generated one after a few
steps. Larger \code{oversample} increases the bias in each step (more short responses compete for
the $B$ slots) but also increases throughput; the steady-state mixed-policy fraction grows with it.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.8 (Weight sync across sites).}
Qwen3-32B has $\approx32.8$B parameters: bf16 $\approx65.5$\,GB, i.e.\ $\approx5.2$\,s at
12.5\,GB/s; fp8 $\approx2.6$\,s. LoRA rank 64 on all seven projections of 64 layers is
$64\cdot64\cdot\sum(d_{\text{in}}+d_{\text{out}})\approx 537$M parameters, $\approx1.07$\,GB in bf16,
$\approx0.09$\,s. With full weights, use bounded-staleness asynchronous RL so the 5\,s transfer overlaps
generation; with LoRA, even a synchronous schedule pays little.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.9 (Attacking the grader).}
(1) \code{os.\_exit(0)} or \code{sys.exit(0)} before the tests run---run tests in a separate process
that imports the candidate, and require a sentinel printed after all tests; (2) returning an object
whose \code{\_\_eq\_\_} always returns \code{True}---compare with type checks or serialise results;
(3) monkeypatching \code{assert}-like helpers, \code{unittest} or builtins---execute tests in a fresh
namespace after the candidate code and verify the runner's integrity; (4) reading expected outputs
from disk or environment---keep tests outside the sandbox, mount nothing, empty environment;
(5) exhausting memory/CPU so the grader crashes into a default---treat every abnormal exit as failure
and count resources. Writing (1) and (2) as tests against \cref{lst:rlsys:sandbox} shows that the
teaching sandbox is vulnerable to both.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.10 (Measurement).}
An \texttt{async+partial} policy runs the partial-rollout generator in its own pool while the
previous batch trains. Expect throughput above both \texttt{async} and \texttt{partial} for heavy
tails, with mean staleness between 1 and 2 and a mixed-policy fraction similar to \texttt{partial};
throughput rises and saturates with \code{oversample} while staleness and length bias grow.

\paragraph{Exercise 39.11 (Design).}
One reasonable answer: 14B bf16 weights are $\approx29$\,GB, KV $\approx 2\cdot40\cdot8\cdot128\cdot2
\approx164$\,KB/token. Responses average 6k tokens and are capped at 32k, so the tail is severe:
use split pools with bounded-staleness async plus partial rollouts. Generation: 12 GPUs as TP$=2$
engines (6 replicas, $\sim$100\,GB KV each for $\sim$100--150 concurrent long sequences per replica);
training: 20 GPUs with FSDP (16\,B/param $\Rightarrow$ 224\,GB of state, $\approx11$\,GB per GPU) plus
activation checkpointing and token-budget packing. Balance the pools by measuring $W_g$ and $W_t$
in the first steps, drop the reference model (KL$=0$) to save memory, enable TIS and log mismatch,
and stream rewards to a CPU verifier pool.
